# BUSINESS B13 — Build-ready product contract

## 1. Product boundary and inherited decisions

**Product:** RFP Shred (market label: Shred-to-Inbox).
**User:** The founder, owner, or capture manager of a 5–50-person set-aside federal shop — SDVOSB, 8(a), HUBZone, or WOSB — pursuing IT and professional-services solicitations.
**Job:** For digital PDFs, within minutes (target ≤5 minutes); for scanned documents, within 15 minutes — convert a solicitation's PDF, DOCX, or ZIP package into a draft compliance matrix where every line cites an exact page and clause and carries the full requirement text, displacing the 6–12-hour weekend of manual shredding. The nightly sweep surfaces new opportunities matching the founder's own NAICS and set-aside watchlist — it is the founder's alert tool, not an automated outreach machine, and it is not the end user's upload path.

**Competitive positioning note:** This product is a repackaging of well-known extraction techniques (chunked LLM summarization, OCR, citation audit) optimized for cost and solo-founder operation in the federal set-aside niche. The differentiation thesis — lower price, self-serve checkout, page-cited compliance matrices for small govcon — is **not a verified fact**. No competitive map (B8) was supplied; the product may duplicate an existing solved problem. This risk is carried as A-011 and is prominently flagged here, not buried in an appendix.

**Upstream artifacts surfaced.** B0 operator intent and B6 concept lock are quoted directly from the supplied upstream text. No B1, B8, B9, B10, B11, or B12 standalone artifact was supplied; each absence is named, not papered over.

> **B0 operator intent (quoted):** Goal: Ship a niche B2B SaaS micro-tool to a live, paying beta within 12 weeks, reaching first revenue on under $5k of spend as a solo technical founder. Team capability: solo. Risk tolerance: balanced. Walk-aways: (a) requires more than $5k of upfront capital before first revenue, (b) cannot reach a working MVP with a solo founder, (c) depends on a regulated domain (medical, financial, or legal advice), (d) has no credible path to a paying customer within 12 weeks, (e) core value depends entirely on one third-party platform that can cut access.

> **B6 concept lock (quoted):** Concept: RFP-Shred-to-Inbox — compliance matrices for small government contractors.

> **B7 customer model (quoted, `model_inference` provenance):** Primary segment: owner-capture leads at 5–50-person set-aside govcon bidding IT and professional services. WTP hypothesis: $149–$249. Decision maker: owner or capture lead (3-day cycle). Top JTBD: extract Section L and Section M requirements into a draft compliance matrix within minutes of RFP release.

### Scope decision: vendor outreach deferred to v2

The build seed proposed a nightly sweep that auto-emails sample matrices to SAM.gov-registered vendors. This contract **defers vendor outreach to v2** and retains only the founder-watchlist alert loop. This is a **scope decision, not a "reframing"**: the original DIS-10 all-vendor outreach position is not resolved — it is cut from v1. The reasons are concrete:

1. **$5k cap and 12-week solo constraint:** The vendor-outreach loop requires a vendor registry sync (SAM.gov Entity Management API), packet generation, claim-link mechanics, unsubscribe infrastructure, and CAN-SPAM compliance. Each is a distinct build surface. At solo-founder velocity, building both loops risks delivering neither.
2. **Legal risk:** The build seed itself makes outreach contingent on legal clearance. Shipping a `manual` outreach default still requires the packet-generation pipeline and claim mechanics to exist. Deferring eliminates the liability surface entirely.
3. **Core value independence:** The core loop (upload → matrix → export) does not depend on outreach. The founder-watchlist alert (surfacing relevant RFPs in the founder's library) directly serves C-005 without any vendor contact.

The founder can still perform manual outreach by viewing matched RFPs in their library and contacting vendors directly using a generated sample matrix — the extraction pipeline runs regardless. Automated distribution is v2. This decision drops a feature (vendor packets, claim links, unsubscribe) and keeps a feature (founder watchlist alerts with daily digest email); the cut is owned here, not papered over.

### Two surfaces, one pipeline — explicit separation

The build seed's deliverables serve two economically distinct surfaces that share one extraction pipeline but have different customers and compliance profiles.

**Core surface (self-serve — the product the user pays for):**
1. Solicitation-in, matrix-out: upload (PDF, DOCX, or ZIP of multiple documents) → machine read → draft matrix; ten lines free, remainder paid.
2. A review checklist a human must clear line by line; export stays shut while any line is unchecked. Noise-filter drops and citation-audit drops are both surfaced for review and re-inclusion. Re-included audit-drops undergo a fresh citation audit (FR-040).
3. One-click export of a cleared matrix to Excel (.xlsx) and Word (.docx) with six columns: Clause ID · Section · Source Page · Requirement Text · Response · Checked.
4. Revenue enforcement inside the app: $149 to open one solicitation's matrix, or $249 per month for unlimited openings during beta. Once a matrix is opened it stays open (FR-015); re-upload of the same file by the same account inherits the unlock (FR-033). A per-account monthly inference ceiling suspends processing when exceeded (FR-039).

**Founder alert surface (founder productivity — no vendor contact in v1):**
5. A nightly pull of new federal postings matched against the founder's own NAICS/set-aside watchlist — surfacing relevant RFPs to the founder as sample entries in their library, directly serving C-005. A daily digest email (FR-044) notifies the founder of new matches, using existing SMTP infrastructure. No vendor registry, no packets, no automated outreach. The founder can manually share sample matrices with vendors if they choose; the tool does not facilitate or automate it.

The conflation is justified: both surfaces reuse the identical FR-005–FR-009 pipeline and data model, and the sweep is a zero-marginal-cost channel under the $5k cap (distribution table above).

**OUT scope (no FR may be written against these):** proposal drafting, writing, or compliance answer generation; opportunity search or CRM; teaming discovery; multi-user workspaces; state or local portal ingest; third-party integrations beyond Stripe, SAM.gov opportunities API, a model provider, and SMTP; customer-facing API (FR-032 is workbench data for the owner's own matrix, not a public API); **automated vendor outreach, vendor packets, claim links, and unsubscribe flows (deferred to v2 per the §1 scope decision)**; amendment diffing (FR-042 provides manual linking only, not automated delta views); row reordering drag-and-drop UI (data model supports mutable `seq` and `tags`; UI deferred to v2).

**Known limitations (not OUT scope, but not fully built in this slice):** multi-file solicitations with individual attachment tracking (FR-041 accepts ZIP and merges server-side into one processing PDF but does not track individual file boundaries; merge order is filename-sorted and may not match logical document order — carried as A-012); amendment diffing (FR-042 stores `amends_rfp_id` for manual linking — data-model readiness, no diff view); row reordering UI (`seq` is mutable via FR-016 PATCH; the workbench renders document order). All three are bounded v1 decisions carried with validation tasks (A-012, A-019), not oversights.

## 2. Product surfaces

Everything user-facing is server-rendered HTML (Jinja2 + htmx) with a parallel read/write JSON API (FR-032) for future decoupling. The constraint is "no JS build pipeline" — `htmx.min.js` is a ~50KB vendored static asset, not a compiled bundle. Global obligations: WCAG 2.2 AA floor — semantic landmarks, labeled form controls, visible focus indicators, ≥4.5:1 contrast ratio, full keyboard operability, `aria-live="polite"` on progress regions — and legibility down to 360px width. Every state-changing form and htmx request carries a CSRF token (FR-029). For JSON API clients that cannot parse HTML hidden fields, the CSRF token is also returned in the `POST /login` and `POST /signup` JSON response body, and a dedicated `GET /api/csrf` endpoint (authenticated) provides token retrieval for existing sessions (FR-029). An automated axe-core scan (AC-028) verifies the structural baseline after representative interactions; full WCAG conformance testing is a post-beta task.

**HTMX workbench strategy (DIS-1 resolution).** S6 uses per-row htmx targets: check toggles and response edits are row-level PATCHes returning the updated row fragment; the verification counter and export-lock state update via `hx-swap-oob` out-of-band swaps; focus is preserved on the active row via `hx-preserve`/`hx-select`; citation excerpts load as htmx-driven tooltips. **Tag editing:** each row displays its tags as inline pill-shaped labels; clicking a tag pill removes it (PATCH with updated tag array); an inline text input at the end of the tag row accepts new tags on Enter (PATCH appending the new tag to the existing `tags` jsonb array). No modal or separate tag-editing surface — editing is inline and immediate. **Concurrent PATCH serialization:** each row's input controls carry `hx-disabled-elt` targeting themselves during the PATCH — the control is disabled until the server responds, preventing concurrent PATCHes from desynchronizing the DOM. If the PATCH fails (network error, 403, session timeout), the control reverts to its prior state and an inline error appears with an `aria-live="polite"` announcement ("Save failed — please retry"); an `aria-live` announcement also confirms each successful save. No optimistic UI; the DOM reflects server truth. The server processes PATCHes sequentially per row; the export-lock check re-evaluates on every PATCH. No full-page re-render occurs during review. The htmx frontend is the default consumer of the FR-032 JSON API, not the only possible consumer.

**S1 — Home (`/`, anonymous).** Purpose: pitch, sample screenshot, pricing (including the FR-039 monthly inference-cap disclosure for the $249 plan and the per-account usage widget). In: none. Out: CTA to `/signup`. States: static. Permissions: public. Accessibility: semantic landmarks, alt text on the screenshot.

**S2 — Auth (`/signup`, `/login`, `/logout`, `/forgot-password`, `/reset-password`).** In: email + password (≥12 chars). Out: session cookie, redirect to `/app`. Errors: duplicate email (409); uniform "invalid credentials" (401) for any mismatch; 429 after ten failed logins per five minutes per IP. Password reset (step 1): email in, always 200 out (no account enumeration); if the account exists, a signed single-use token (1-hour TTL) is emailed via the existing SMTP infrastructure. Reset (step 2): token + new password (≥12 chars) in; password updated, all sessions revoked, redirect to `/login`; 410 for expired/used/forged tokens. Permissions: anonymous; authenticated users redirect to the library. Accessibility: labeled inputs; errors associated via `aria-describedby`.

**S3 — Library (`/app`).** Purpose: the account's solicitations with state chips (`received`, `reading`, `extracting`, `auditing`, `ready`, `unlocked`, `cleared`, `exported`, `failed`, `expired`) and dates, newest first. Founder-watchlist matches from Journey B appear here as sample entries (badge "Watchlist match", `origin='sweep'`). If the latest sweep produced new matches, a banner at the top reads "N new watchlist matches from last night's sweep" with `aria-live="polite"` and deep links to each. Rows with `amends_rfp_id` set show an "Amendment" badge linking to the parent matrix. Rows with `stored_path IS NOT NULL` (source file still retained within the 24-hour grace period) show a "Re-process" button (FR-045). Empty state: "Shred your first RFP" CTA with a brief product-value summary. Loading: skeleton rows during initial fetch. Error: "Could not load library — retry" with refresh button. Inference-cap state: when FR-039 suspends processing, a banner reads "Your monthly processing limit has been reached — uploads will resume on the 1st." Permissions: own rows only; server-side `account_id` filter enforced. Accessibility: table semantics with `scope` attributes; state chips exposed as `aria-label` text; empty state has `role="status"`.

**S4 — Intake (`/app/new`).** In: one file (`.pdf`, `.docx`, or `.zip` containing PDF/DOCX files, ≤ `MAX_UPLOAD_MB` default 60), checkbox "Keep the source file after processing" (default off), optional dropdown "Is this an amendment to a previous solicitation?" listing the account's prior matrices (FR-042). Out: redirect to S5. Errors inline: 413 oversize (Content-Length checked before the body streams), 415 wrong type, 422 unreadable or encrypted — nothing persisted on rejection. ZIP files containing non-PDF/DOCX entries are rejected with "ZIP must contain only PDF or DOCX files" (FR-041). ZIP files exceeding `MAX_ZIP_ENTRIES` (default 500) are rejected with 422 "ZIP contains too many files (max 500)" (FR-041). Permissions: authenticated; rate-limited per FR-030. Accessibility: drag-drop zone with a keyboard-operable file input, labeled; amendment dropdown labeled with `aria-describedby`.

**S5 — Progress (`/app/jobs/{id}`).** Purpose: live stage display with page counter; htmx refreshes every two seconds from `GET /api/jobs/{id}`. Stage transitions announced via `aria-live="polite"`: "Reading document…", "Extracting requirements…", "Auditing citations…", "Ready." Error state: `failed` plus plain-language cause (including specific failure messages such as "Document exceeded maximum chunk count (60). This RFP may be too long or too dense for the current chunking strategy. Try splitting the document or contact support." or "max retries exceeded — worker repeatedly died on this document") and a Retry button. If the source file is still retained (within 24h grace), a "Re-process from source" button (FR-045) appears alongside Retry. **Degradation state (FR-043):** if both model providers are unreachable, shows "Processing is temporarily paused — your document is queued and will process when service resumes" with the job in `queued` state. Permissions: owner; foreign IDs return 404 (no existence leak, wording identical for "foreign" and "nonexistent"). Accessibility: `aria-live="polite"` progress region, `role="status"` on the stage label; Retry button keyboard-focusable with an `aria-label` including the job ID.

**S6 — Workbench (`/app/matrices/{id}`).** Purpose: the core surface. Table of requirement lines: clause ID, section, cited page (hover/focus shows the matching excerpt), requirement text, response field, checkbox, tags (jsonb, editable inline via PATCH — pill labels with click-to-remove and an inline text input for adding new tags on Enter). Without entitlement: exactly ten lines (or all lines if total ≤ 10 — see FR-012), a locked-row count integer, and an unlock CTA — **locked rows are excluded from the template context entirely** (FR-012); the template receives only `preview_rows` (≤10) and `locked_count`. When total ≤ 10, all rows are visible, `locked_count=0`, and no unlock CTA renders — the matrix is free; export unlocks after full checking. With entitlement: all lines, live checkboxes, counter ("Verified 61 / 63"). **Mid-session unlock:** the workbench polls `/api/matrices/{id}` on `visibilitychange` (tab focus regain) to detect entitlement changes; if the matrix was unlocked while the user was away, the page auto-reloads with all rows. A banner reports citation-audit drops ("N lines could not be verified against source text and were excluded"). A collapsible "Review N filtered lines" panel (FR-034) lists noise-filter drops with page, 80-character preview, full text (expandable), filter reason, and a per-line "Re-include" button. A collapsible "Review N audit-dropped lines" panel (FR-040) lists citation-audit failures with page, excerpt, full text (expandable), audit-failure reason, and a per-line "Re-include" button. **Annotation mode (FR-038):** for `is_staff` accounts, a "Ground Truth" toggle reveals per-row buttons: "Correct binding," "Not a requirement," "Add missing requirement." Zero-row empty state: "No binding requirements detected — verify the document contains Section L or M," with the unlock CTA hidden (FR-013). Permissions: owner; line visibility enforced server-side; PATCH on locked row IDs returns 404 (FR-016). Accessibility: `scope`-attributed headers; each checkbox `aria-label` includes its clause ID; focus managed on re-include; panels are `aria-expanded` disclosure widgets; fully keyboard navigable.

**S7 — Checkout handoff (`/app/matrices/{id}/unlock` → Stripe-hosted).** Purpose: pick $149 single or $249 monthly. Out: Stripe Checkout; return to `/billing/return?m={id}`, which polls entitlement and forwards to S6. Error: canceled checkout lands back on S6 with a notice. AND created_at >= date_trunc('month', now()::timestamp at time zone 'UTC')`), delete-matrix, delete-account. Permissions: owner. ## 3. Primary end-to-end journey

**Journey A — customer, first visit to exported matrix.**

1. **Arrive.** Anonymous visitor hits S1 → `/signup` → account row created (argon2id hash) → S3 empty library. Durable: `accounts` row.

2. **Intake.** On S4 the user drops `solicitation.zip` (containing the base RFP PDF plus two PDF attachments and one DOCX amendment, total ~200 pages), leaves retention off, optionally selects an amendment link. The server checks Content-Length before streaming (early 413), verifies the header signature (`%PDF` / `PK\x03\x04`), computes the SHA-256 content hash of the uploaded bytes, and attempts a pikepdf empty-password open on PDFs (encrypted PDFs that resist open fail fast with 422). For ZIP files, entries are validated (count ≤ `MAX_ZIP_ENTRIES`, types `.pdf`/`.docx` only, decompression bomb check). If the same account already holds an `rfps` row with this hash and `unlocked_at IS NOT NULL`, the new row inherits `unlocked_at` (FR-033 — no payment). Otherwise: `rfps` row (`received`, `retain_source=false`, `content_hash=<sha256>`, `origin='user'`, `amends_rfp_id=<selected or NULL>`), bytes written to `FILES_DIR/incoming/{uuid}` — never a user-supplied name. Transition: `received → reading`. Failure: corrupt file → 422, row marked `failed`, temp bytes wiped; re-upload costs nothing.

3. **Processing.** The worker claims the job atomically (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, priority-ordered), spawns a heartbeat daemon thread that uses a **dedicated SQLAlchemy engine with `NullPool`** (thread-safe; no shared connection with the main thread) and updates `extraction_runs.heartbeat_at` every 15 seconds — the thread survives blocking LibreOffice/OCR calls and dies with the process — then: builds the page map (pdfplumber per-page text; any page under 50 extractable characters is flagged for OCR; all flagged pages are extracted into a temporary sub-PDF, OCR'd in a single `ocrmypdf` pass, and merged back — not invoked per-page in a loop; **OCR'd text is mapped back to the original page numbers in the source document, not the sub-PDF's 1..N page numbers** — the page-map maintains a `sub_pdf_page → original_page` mapping throughout the OCR and merge process; OCR'd pages yielding fewer than 10 characters are flagged as OCR-failed and counted; DOCX inputs were already converted to PDF during intake so citations match rendered pagination), mines requirements via section-aware chunking with hierarchical context (each chunk includes its parent section heading and a summary of preceding sections for cross-reference awareness), stores raw model output as JSONB in `raw_model_outputs`, derives requirement rows via a transform layer that includes `clause_id` normalization (lowercasing, stripping trailing punctuation, converting whitespace runs to `.`), audits each row's excerpt against the cited page's normalized text (FR-008), writes noise-filter drops to `filtered_lines` with **full text preserved** (not just 80-char preview) and citation-audit drops to `audit_drops` (FR-040), and persists surviving rows in deterministic document order. Transitions: `reading → extracting → auditing → ready`. Failure: provider 429/5xx → three backed-off retries → `failed` + Retry on S5; worker death → on boot, any run heartbeat-silent over ten minutes is re-queued: `pg_try_advisory_lock(hashtextextended(run_id::text, 0))` is attempted (non-blocking; returns false if held), and if acquired, the `jobs` row is reset, `extraction_runs.attempt` is incremented, and partial rows are cleaned — **all wrapped in a single database transaction** to prevent partial state on concurrent re-queue attempts. **Maximum 3 attempts** — after the third attempt, the run is marked `failed` with cause `"max retries exceeded — worker repeatedly died on this document"` and the file retained under the failure-retention rule (7-day TTL). Durable: matrix rows in Postgres — progress readable from any device.

4. **Preview.** S6 serves exactly ten lines (or all lines if total ≤ 10) with page-cited excerpts plus "53 further requirements locked — open the full matrix." If total ≤ 10, all rows are visible and no unlock CTA renders (FR-012). Rows 11+ are absent from the template context and from the FR-032 JSON — the locked remainder is a number, not hidden markup. The filtered-lines panel and audit-drops panel are both visible. If zero requirements were extracted, the empty state shows and no unlock CTA renders.

5. **Pay.** S7 → Stripe Checkout → webhook `checkout.session.completed` (signature-checked, idempotent, both operations — entitlement creation and `unlocked_at` update — wrapped in a single database transaction) → entitlement row + `rfps.unlocked_at` set → S6 shows everything. If the matrix was unlocked between a PATCH request's arrival and processing (mid-session unlock), the PATCH evaluates entitlement at processing time and succeeds (FR-016). Failure: abandoned checkout → no entitlement, preview intact; duplicate webhook → one entitlement only (transaction rolls back on replay; idempotency key prevents double execution). A zero-requirement matrix or a matrix with ≤10 requirements cannot be checked out (409).

6. **Check and review.** The user ticks each line (every toggle a persisted CSRF-protected PATCH; the row control disables during the request and re-enables on response to prevent concurrent PATCH desynchronization). Counter: "Checked 61 / 63." The user opens the filtered-lines panel and audit-drops panel and re-includes any wrongly dropped lines; re-included lines join the checklist with their full text. Re-included audit-drops undergo a fresh citation audit (FR-040) — if the audit passes, the row persists with a verified excerpt; if it fails again, the row returns to `audit_drops` with an updated reason. Un-checking is allowed any time; export re-locks if any row is unchecked.

7. **Export — the visible success state.** With all lines checked, a green banner reads "63 / 63 checked — export unlocked." Buttons deliver `matrix-{solicitation}.xlsx` and `matrix-{solicitation}.docx`, each opening on six contract columns: Clause ID · Section · Source Page · Requirement Text · Response · Checked. The export is self-contained — shareable without the original solicitation. Transition: `cleared → exported` (re-export allowed). Post-condition: retention was off, so the source file enters the 24-hour grace period; within it the user may re-trigger extraction via the "Re-process" button on S3 or S5 (FR-045) at no cost; afterwards the file is destroyed and a `retention_log` row written. Matrix rows persist regardless.

8. **Any restart anywhere** loses nothing: account, rows, checks, filtered lines, audit drops, entitlement all durable in PostgreSQL. Sessions live 7 days sliding; a mid-review expiry redirects to `/login` with a return URL, and the user resumes with state intact.

**Journey B — operator, founder watchlist to alert.**

1. **Watchlist setup.** The founder sets their NAICS codes and set-aside designations in S9; these drive founder-mode matching (FR-022) and personalize the sweep to the founder's own business profile.

2. **Sweep.** Scheduler (`SWEEP_CRON`, default 06:00 UTC) queries `GET https://api.sam.gov/opportunities/v2/search` for the trailing 24 hours (`postedFrom`/`postedTo`, `limit=1000`, `offset` pagination until a short page), throttled to `SAM_MAX_RPS` (default 2). Every call is individually wrapped — a dead call is logged into `sweep_runs.errors` and skipped; the sweep always finishes. Deduplication via `sam_notices.notice_id` PK upsert. Backfill after an outage runs in 24-hour chunks up to 7 days; beyond that, `scripts/backfill_sam.py --days N`. Durable: `sweep_runs`.

3. **Match (founder watchlist only).** Postings where NAICS sets intersect the founder's `watch_naics` and the posting's set-aside appears in `watch_set_asides` generate a sample matrix: **a `jobs` row is enqueued (priority=1) for the `worker` to process — the sweep container does not run the pipeline synchronously.** The posting attachment is fetched via the SAM.gov response's `resourceLinks` or `responseUrl` field (with fallback to the posting's `uiLink` page for manual download); the fetched bytes are validated against `MAX_UPLOAD_MB` and header-signature checked (`%PDF`, `PK\x03\x04`) before processing, identical to FR-004 intake validation; oversize or invalid attachments are logged to `sweep_runs.errors` and skipped without touching siblings. The resulting `rfps` row is owned by the founder's account with `origin='sweep'` and `retain_source=false` (FR-020's 24-hour grace applies identically). **Per-run cap:** `MAX_SWEEP_MATCHES_PER_RUN` (default 50) limits matches per nightly sweep; excess matches are logged to `sweep_runs.errors` with `excess_matches_skipped` and processed on the next night's run. **Sweep jobs are exempt from the per-account inference cap (FR-039)** — the founder does not consume their own inference budget for watchlist matches. The system-wide $200/month aggregate cost ceiling (§8) guards total sweep spend. No vendor registry, no packets, no outreach.

4. **Digest notification.** After all matches for the run are processed (or `MAX_SWEEP_MATCHES_PER_RUN` reached), if one or more matches were produced, a digest email (FR-044) is sent to the founder's registered email: "[RFP Shred] N new watchlist match(es)" with one line per match (solicitation number, title, due date, deep link to the sample matrix in S3).

5. **Use.** The founder views the matched RFP in their library (S3 shows the "N new watchlist matches" banner), opens the ten-row preview, and can unlock it for $149 or via their $249 plan — the sample matrix is a fully functional RFP Shred matrix with no special packet or claim mechanics.

## 4. Functional contract

- **FR-001** — Signup: `POST /signup` with email + password (≥12 chars) creates an `accounts` row (argon2id hash) and opens a server-side session; 303 to `/app` with `Set-Cookie` (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax); 409 on duplicate email, 422 on weak password; a per-session CSRF token is issued with the session and returned in the JSON response body for API clients; needs Postgres.

- **FR-002** — Login/logout: `POST /login` checks credentials with uniform failure wording and constant-time comparison, issues a server-side session (7-day sliding, FR-031); `POST /logout` revokes it; 401 "invalid credentials" for any mismatch; 429 after ten failures per five minutes per IP; the login JSON response includes `csrf_token` for API clients (FR-029); needs FR-001.

- **FR-003** — Library: `GET /app` lists the caller's solicitations with state chips, newest first, strictly ownership-scoped; watchlist sample entries (`origin='sweep'`) carry a "Watchlist match" badge; if the latest sweep produced new matches, a banner reads "N new watchlist matches from last night's sweep" with deep links; rows with `amends_rfp_id` carry an "Amendment" badge; rows with `stored_path IS NOT NULL` show a "Re-process" button (FR-045); empty-state CTA at zero rows; anonymous callers redirect to `/login`; needs FR-001.

- **FR-004** — Intake: `POST /app/new` accepts `.pdf`, `.docx`, or `.zip` (containing PDF/DOCX files only, ≤ `MAX_ZIP_ENTRIES` default 500 entries) up to `MAX_UPLOAD_MB` (default 60), verified by header signature (`%PDF`, `PK\x03\x04`); Content-Length checked before the body streams (early 413); SHA-256 content hash of the uploaded bytes computed and stored; encrypted PDFs attempted via pikepdf empty-password open, else 422 with "This PDF is encrypted and cannot be processed"; ZIP files handled per FR-041 (entry count, type, and bomb checks at intake; DOCX conversion and PDF merge deferred to the worker pipeline per FR-005 to avoid cross-container LibreOffice contention); optional `amends_rfp_id` from a dropdown listing the account's prior matrices (FR-042); creates `rfps` (with `origin='user'`) + `extraction_runs` + `jobs` rows (priority=10, user-initiated); 413 oversize, 415 wrong type, 422 corrupt/encrypted/invalid-zip/too-many-entries — no bytes kept on rejection; rate-limited per FR-030; same-account re-upload with a matching hash and prior unlock inherits `unlocked_at` per FR-033; needs Postgres.

- **FR-005** — Page-map build: per-page text via pdfplumber; **per-page OCR detection** — any page yielding fewer than 50 extractable characters is flagged for OCR. All flagged pages are extracted into a temporary sub-PDF, OCR'd in a **single `ocrmypdf` pass**, and merged back into the page map — not invoked per-page in a loop, to avoid process-spawn overhead. **OCR'd text is mapped back to the original page numbers in the source document, not the sub-PDF's 1..N page numbers** — the page-map maintains a `sub_pdf_page → original_page` mapping throughout the OCR and merge process. OCR'd pages yielding fewer than 10 characters after OCR are flagged as OCR-failed and counted in the run record (the page contributes no text but does not abort the run). DOCX converts to PDF through headless LibreOffice (serialized via **Postgres advisory lock** `pg_try_advisory_lock(hashtextextended('libreoffice_conversion', 0))` — cross-container safe since both API and worker share the same Postgres; the lock is held for the duration of the conversion and released on completion or process death) so page cites match the rendered PDF's pagination; the Dockerfile installs `fonts-liberation` and `fonts-liberation2` to reduce font-substitution drift, but residual drift from absent Microsoft core fonts (Calibri has no metric-compatible open equivalent) is carried as A-017; emits `{page: text}` and records `page_count`; per-page OCR gaps are logged and skipped; tool errors fail the run with cause; needs FR-004. [ASSUMPTION] A-009 (LibreOffice pagination fidelity), A-002 (Tesseract accuracy), and A-017 (font substitution) carry the validation tasks and fallbacks.

- **FR-006** — Background runner: database-backed queue (no Redis). Workers claim jobs atomically: `UPDATE jobs SET status='processing', locked_at=now(), locked_by=$worker_id WHERE id = (SELECT id FROM jobs WHERE status='queued' ORDER BY priority DESC, enqueued_at ASC LIMIT 1 FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED) RETURNING *`. User-initiated jobs carry priority=10; sweep-generated sample matrices priority=1, so user uploads always preempt queued sweep work. A heartbeat daemon thread uses a **dedicated SQLAlchemy engine with `NullPool`** (thread-safe; no shared connections with the main thread's pool) and updates `extraction_runs.heartbeat_at` every 15 seconds, persists during blocking LibreOffice/OCR calls, and dies with the process. Stale re-queue at boot: any run heartbeat-silent over ten minutes is re-queued under `SELECT pg_try_advisory_lock(hashtextextended(run_id::text, 0))` — a non-blocking call that returns false immediately if the lock is held, allowing the caller to skip (no double-processing). If the lock is acquired, the re-queue resets the `jobs` row: `status='queued'`, `locked_at=NULL`, `locked_by=NULL`, **increments `extraction_runs.attempt = attempt + 1`**, and cleans partial output: `DELETE FROM requirements WHERE rfp_id=? AND verified_at IS NULL; DELETE FROM filtered_lines WHERE rfp_id=?; DELETE FROM audit_drops WHERE rfp_id=?; DELETE FROM raw_model_outputs WHERE extraction_run_id=?`. **The cleanup, re-queue reset, and attempt increment are wrapped in a single database transaction** to prevent partial state on concurrent re-queue attempts. **Maximum 3 attempts** — after the third attempt, the run is marked `failed` with cause `"max retries exceeded — worker repeatedly died on this document"` and the file retained under the failure-retention rule (7-day TTL). This cleanup runs regardless of failure cause (provider error, worker crash, OOM). Three backed-off retries on provider 429/5xx (retries target the configured provider only — no automatic failover to the secondary adapter; see FR-011 and A-023). `WORKER_CONCURRENCY` (default 1) bounds concurrent jobs per worker; compose `deploy.replicas: 1` for the worker service prevents multi-container file-lock bypass. Target: ~200-page digital PDF done in ≤5 minutes, full-OCR path ≤15 minutes on the reference host (4 vCPU / 8 GB); needs FR-005, model provider.

- **FR-007** — Requirement mining: section-aware chunking with hierarchical context (Sections L and M prioritized, other obligation-bearing parts included). Chunking strategy: (1) identify section boundaries via heading and numbering patterns ("Section L", "L.3", "M.1.a"); (2) target ~6,000 characters per chunk with ~500-character overlap at boundaries; (3) each chunk carries section heading, page range, chunk text, chunk index, and a **section-context summary** (parent section heading + one-sentence summary of preceding sections) for cross-reference awareness; chunks processed independently and merged. **Safety bounds:** maximum 60 chunks per RFP (run fails with cause `"Document exceeded maximum chunk count (60). This RFP may be too long or too dense for the current chunking strategy. Try splitting the document or contact support."` beyond that); maximum 25 requirements per chunk — if exceeded, the chunk is halved and processed as two sub-chunks. **Dynamic chunk sizing:** if a chunk's estimated output (estimated requirements × ~500 tokens/requirement) would exceed `MODEL_MAX_TOKENS`, the chunk is halved preemptively. **Truncation detection:** if the model response has `finish_reason == 'length'` (or `usage.completion_tokens >= MODEL_MAX_TOKENS - 100`), the chunk is halved and re-processed as two sub-chunks; maximum re-chunk depth 2 (original → half → quarter), after which the sub-chunk fails and is counted while the run completes. Each chunk goes to the model with this strict JSON schema:

```json
{
  "type": "object",
  "required": ["requirements"],
  "properties": {
    "requirements": {
      "type": "array",
      "items": {
        "type": "object",
        "required": ["clause_id", "section", "page", "text", "excerpt", "is_requirement"],
        "properties": {
          "clause_id": {"type": "string", "description": "Requirement identifier, e.g., L.3.2.1 or M.1.a"},
          "section": {"type": "string", "description": "Section heading, e.g., Section L"},
          "page": {"type": "integer", "minimum": 1},
          "text": {"type": "string", "maxLength": 2000, "description": "Full requirement text"},
          "excerpt": {"type": "string", "maxLength": 200, "description": "Verbatim quote from the source page containing the requirement"},
          "is_requirement": {"type": "boolean", "description": "true if binding requirement; false if boilerplate/TOC/narrative"}
        },
        "additionalProperties": false
      }
    }
  },
  "additionalProperties": false
}
```

  System prompt: "You are a compliance matrix extraction system. Extract all requirement-like items from federal solicitations — both binding requirements and non-binding text (boilerplate, table of contents entries, narrative prose). For each item, set `is_requirement=true` if it imposes an obligation on the offeror (identified by modal verbs: shall, must, will, is required to, is responsible for, or imperative mood in instruction sections L, M, and evaluation criteria), and `is_requirement=false` otherwise. Return ALL items you identify — do not omit non-requirements; the downstream system will route non-binding items to a review panel. For each item, provide the clause identifier, section label, page number, the full text, and a short excerpt (≤200 characters) from the source page. Return only JSON matching the provided schema."
  Per-chunk user prompt: "Section: {section_heading}\nParent section: {parent_section}\nPages: {page_start}-{page_end}\nSection context: {section_context_summary}\nContent:\n{chunk_text}\n\nExtract all candidate requirements (both binding and non-binding) from this section. Return JSON per the schema."
  **Raw model output** is stored as JSONB in `raw_model_outputs` (with `extraction_run_id`, `chunk_index`, `tokens_in`, `tokens_out`, `finish_reason`, `prompt_version`) before transformation; requirement rows are derived via a transform layer (`app/pipeline/transform.py`) that maps the JSONB to `requirements` and `filtered_lines` tables, referencing `requirements.model_output_id`. **`clause_id` normalization:** the transform layer normalizes `clause_id` values via `text_utils.normalize_clause_id()` — lowercasing, stripping trailing punctuation, converting whitespace runs and hyphens to `.`, and collapsing consecutive dots. This ensures `L.3.2.1`, `L 3 2 1`, `L.3-2-1`, and `L.3.2.1.` all produce the same normalized form. The normalization is idempotent (applying it twice produces the same result). This allows prompt/schema iteration without schema churn. Malformed output (non-truncation) retries its chunk once, then that chunk fails and is counted while the run completes. Output is capped at `MODEL_MAX_TOKENS` (default 8192, raised from 4096 to accommodate dense sections) per chunk request; needs FR-006, FR-011.

- **FR-008** — Citation audit: each mined row's excerpt must verify against the cited page's text. Normalization: collapse repeated whitespace and trim; lowercase; replace common OCR ligature artifacts (ﬁ→fi, ﬂ→fl); join hyphenated line breaks (word-\n→word). Containment: the normalized excerpt must be a substring of the normalized page text OR match with Levenshtein distance ratio ≤0.10 (fuzzy match absorbing OCR noise). Failures are written to `audit_drops` (not silently discarded) with `rfp_id`, `seq`, `page`, `excerpt`, `text` (full model output text), and `audit_failure_reason`. Failures are counted and surfaced as a banner on S6 plus a review panel (FR-040); needs FR-007.

- **FR-009** — Noise filter + persistence: rows where `is_requirement=false` are written to the `filtered_lines` table (not silently discarded) with `seq`, `page`, `text_preview` (first 80 chars for display), **`full_text`** (complete requirement text for re-inclusion), and `filter_reason`. Citation-audit failures are written to `audit_drops` (FR-008). Surviving rows (`is_requirement=true` and citation-verified) persist in deterministic document order (`seq`) in `requirements` with normalized `clause_id` values. A solicitation yielding zero obligations lands in `ready` with an explanatory empty state, not an error; needs FR-008.

- **FR-010** — Job status: `GET /api/jobs/{id}` returns `{stage, pages_done, pages_total}` JSON for the owner; 404 for foreign or unknown IDs; needs FR-006.

- **FR-011** — Provider neutrality: all model calls pass through a client interface selected by `MODEL_PROVIDER`/`MODEL_NAME`; two reference adapters ship (OpenAI and Anthropic); nothing outside the adapter knows which vendor serves the request; swapping provider is configuration only (manual config change, not automatic runtime failover); output per chunk capped by `MODEL_MAX_TOKENS` (default 8192). **No automatic runtime failover to the secondary adapter** — if the configured provider is unreachable, FR-006 retries that provider only; FR-043 queued-degradation triggers when both are unreachable. [ASSUMPTION] A-023: single-provider outage is acceptable during beta; manual config change switches to the secondary adapter. Two adapters reduce but do not eliminate external LLM dependency (§1 walk-away screen, A-008); needs nothing upstream.

- **FR-012** — Preview gate: responses for a matrix without entitlement contain exactly the first ten rows by `seq` plus a locked-row count integer (`max(0, total_rows - 10)`). **When total rows ≤ 10, all rows are visible, `locked_count=0`, and no unlock CTA renders** — the user gets the full matrix free (export still requires full checking per FR-017). Locked rows (seq > 10) are excluded from the `render_template()` context entirely — not passed as template variables, not rendered as hidden markup, not present in any JSON island or prefetch. The FR-032 JSON endpoint applies the same rule. No locked row's content appears in any payload; needs FR-009, FR-015.

- **FR-013** — Checkout: `POST /app/matrices/{id}/unlock` creates a Stripe Checkout session for the $149 single-matrix price or the $249 monthly plan (price IDs from env); success/cancel URLs route through `/billing/return`; CSRF-protected. Returns 409 when the matrix has zero requirements or total rows ≤ 10 (all already visible) — no payment for an empty or already-free matrix; needs Stripe keys.

- **FR-014** — Webhook: `POST /stripe/webhook` verifies the Stripe signature, stores each event by provider ID for idempotency, and processes: `checkout.session.completed` (grant entitlement + set `rfps.unlocked_at` — **both operations wrapped in a single database transaction; partial failure rolls back entirely**), `invoice.paid` (extend plan `period_end`), `invoice.payment_failed` (mark plan `active=false`, send dunning email via SMTP), `customer.subscription.deleted` (mark plan `active=false`, set `period_end=now()`), `customer.subscription.updated` (sync status and `period_end`). Each operation is individually idempotent (checks existing state before acting); if an event was dead-lettered, a replay re-attempts full processing (the transaction ensures clean state). Replays and out-of-order delivery never duplicate or revoke incorrectly. Processing failures retry with exponential backoff up to 3 times, then dead-letter to `audit_events`. Unsigned payloads get 400; unhandled event types are stored but not processed. **Beta limitation:** `charge.dispute.created` and `charge.refunded` events are stored but not automatically acted upon; the founder monitors disputes manually via the Stripe dashboard. If a dispute occurs, the founder manually revokes entitlement by updating `entitlements.active=false` via a database command. This is carried as A-022; needs FR-013.

- **FR-015** — Entitlement resolution: a matrix is open iff `rfps.unlocked_at IS NOT NULL` OR the account holds a `single` entitlement for that matrix OR the account holds an active plan (`active=true` AND `period_end` in the future) OR the matrix's total requirement rows ≤ 10 (small matrices are always open per FR-012). **Once `unlocked_at` is set it never reverts** — matrices opened during a plan period stay open after the plan expires; new matrices after expiry require new payment. Evaluation happens per request, server-side; needs FR-014.

- **FR-016** — Row checking + response editing: `PATCH /api/rows/{id}` persists `verified_at` (set or cleared), free-text response edits, and tag updates per row, owner-only, CSRF-protected. **Entitlement is evaluated at processing time, not at request receipt time** — if the matrix is unlocked between request arrival and processing (e.g., webhook fires mid-session), the PATCH succeeds; since `unlocked_at` never reverts, no lock-after-unlock race exists. **Authorization is evaluated in this order, with the same 404 wording returned at every step: (1) row exists? else 404; (2) row belongs to caller's account? else 404; (3) row's matrix is entitled? else 404. No 403 is ever returned except for CSRF mismatch (FR-029).** Returns 404 (not 403) for rows on matrices without entitlement and for rows beyond the preview window — no existence leak, consistent with the foreign-ID doctrine; needs FR-015, FR-029.

- **FR-017** — Export lock: both export routes return 409 with the unchecked count while any row lacks `verified_at`; the workbench disables the buttons and scrolls to the first unchecked row; needs FR-016.

- **FR-018** — Spreadsheet export: `GET /app/matrices/{id}/export.xlsx` streams a workbook whose header row is Clause ID · Section · Source Page · Requirement Text · Response · Checked — six columns, generated on demand from stored rows (including `requirements.body`), never cached on disk; needs FR-017.

- **FR-019** — Document export: `GET /app/matrices/{id}/export.docx` streams a Word file with the same six columns from the same stored rows; needs FR-017.

- **FR-020** — Source lifecycle: on successful extraction with `retain_source=false`, the uploaded file (or merged ZIP output) enters a **24-hour grace period** (not immediate destruction); after 24 hours the file is destroyed and a `retention_log` row written. Within the window the user may re-trigger extraction from the retained file via FR-045 (`POST /app/matrices/{id}/reprocess`). With opt-in the file persists until the user deletes the matrix. A `failed` run keeps the file until the user retries (success starts the 24-hour clock) or discards the matrix. Abandoned failed uploads: a daily cron (`CLEANUP_CRON`, default 03:00 UTC) deletes files whose run has been `failed` for over 7 days, sets `rfps.state='expired'`, and writes a `retention_log` row. **Sweep-origin rows (`origin='sweep'`) default to `retain_source=false`** and FR-020 applies identically — the 24-hour grace, the cleanup cron, and the opt-in option are the same. Matrix rows survive regardless. Exact destruction point: 24 hours after `extraction_runs` reaches `ready` for non-retained files; needs FR-009.

- **FR-021** — Nightly sweep: the scheduler (`SWEEP_CRON`, default 06:00 UTC) queries `GET https://api.sam.gov/opportunities/v2/search` with `postedFrom`/`postedTo` for the trailing 24 h, `limit=1000`, `offset` pagination until a short page, throttled to `SAM_MAX_RPS` (default 2). Every call is individually wrapped — failures append to `sweep_runs.errors` and the run continues to completion. Deduplication via `sam_notices.notice_id` PK upsert. Backfill after an outage runs in 24-hour chunks up to 7 days; beyond that, `scripts/backfill_sam.py --days N`. [ASSUMPTION] A-013 covers API terms for commercial use; A-015 covers the daily quota (~10,000 requests/key). The parser handles unrecognized fields gracefully — renamed or unexpected keys in the SAM.gov response are logged as structured warnings (`sweep_runs.errors` carries `unrecognized_field` entries) and parsing continues with recognized keys. The run record is durable; needs `SAM_API_KEY`.

- **FR-022** — Founder watchlist matching: the founder's `accounts.watch_naics` / `watch_set_asides` (set in S9) are matched against `sam_notices` where NAICS sets intersect and the posting's set-aside appears in the founder's watchlist. Per match: **a `jobs` row is enqueued (priority=1) for the `worker` to process — the sweep container does not run the pipeline synchronously.** The posting attachment is fetched via the SAM.gov response's `resourceLinks` or `responseUrl` field (with fallback to the posting's `uiLink` page for manual download); the fetched bytes are validated against `MAX_UPLOAD_MB` and header-signature checked (`%PDF`, `PK\x03\x04`) before processing, identical to FR-004 intake validation; oversize or invalid attachments are logged to `sweep_runs.errors` and skipped without touching siblings. The resulting `rfps` row is owned by the founder's account with `origin='sweep'` and `retain_source=false` (FR-020 applies). **Per-run cap:** `MAX_SWEEP_MATCHES_PER_RUN` (env, default 50) limits matches per nightly sweep; excess matches are logged to `sweep_runs.errors` with `excess_matches_skipped` and processed on the next night's run. **Sweep jobs are exempt from the per-account inference cap (FR-039)** — the founder does not consume their own inference budget for watchlist matches they did not initiate. The system-wide $200/month aggregate cost ceiling (§8) guards total sweep spend. No vendor registry, no vendor outreach, no Entity Management API calls in v1 (§1 scope decision, DIS-10); needs FR-021.

- **FR-023** — Founder console: `/ops/sweeps`, `/ops/quality`, and `/ops/audit` render run history, match counts, per-call errors, excess matches skipped, quality-monitoring trends, and the append-only audit log (paginated, filterable by action type) for `is_staff` accounts; everyone else gets 404; needs FR-021, FR-026, FR-035.

- **FR-024** — Health: `GET /healthz` returns 200 with `{status, version, db, jobs, model}` only when a trivial query (`SELECT 1`) and a jobs-table probe (`SELECT count(*) FROM jobs WHERE status='queued'`) both succeed; otherwise 503 naming the failed dependency. During FR-043 degradation the payload reports `model: "degraded"`. The checks are dynamic — a hardcoded 200 is a violation.

- **FR-025** — Evaluation harness: `python -m scripts.evaluate_extraction --corpus <dir>` runs the full pipeline over the annotated corpus and prints: **omission rate** = (ground-truth binding requirements not matched by any extracted row at ≥80% token overlap) / (total ground-truth binding requirements); **false-positive rate** = (extracted rows matching no ground-truth requirement at ≥80% token overlap) / (total extracted rows); **page-attribution error rate** = (matched rows where |cited page − ground-truth page| > 1) / (total matched rows). Per-subset metrics (digital vs. scanned vs. DOCX) are reported to isolate OCR-specific and DOCX-specific quality. **The page-attribution threshold is aggregate (all formats combined) rather than per-subset** because AC-031 independently tests DOCX drift and A-002 tests scanned-subset quality. Splitting the harness threshold per format would reduce statistical power on the small corpus (10 RFPs); the aggregate approach with separate per-subset reporting provides the same signal without inflating false negatives on small subsets. **The omission rate is a lower-bound estimate** — the founder's annotations may miss requirements that the model also missed; completeness validation requires a second-pass expert review of a subset (≥3 RFPs) using a different chunking strategy or manual page-by-page review. This is acknowledged as a limitation, not hidden. Exit code 1 when omission exceeds 10%, false positives exceed 20%, or page-attribution error exceeds 5%. Ground-truth format: `{rfp_id}.json` with `{"requirements": [{"clause_id", "section", "page", "text", "is_binding"}]}`; needs FR-009.

- **FR-026** — Audit trail: entitlements granted, exports delivered, mode switches, staff actions, password resets, annotation actions, inference-cap suspensions, and sweep digest emails append to `audit_events` with actor, action, entity, entity_id, and timestamp. The log is append-only — enforced by a PostgreSQL trigger `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE ON audit_events` that raises an exception — and readable only by `is_staff` accounts via `/ops/audit` on S10; retained 12 months. `actor_id` is SET NULL on account deletion.

- **FR-027** — Account self-service: S9 shows plan status, unlock history, watchlist editor, inference usage widget, and amendment links, and lets the owner delete a matrix (rows + any retained source + filtered_lines + audit_drops) or the whole account (cascade delete all `rfps`, `requirements`, `filtered_lines`, `audit_drops`, `entitlements`, `sessions`, `corrections`, `raw_model_outputs`, `corpus_annotations`; `audit_events` retained with `actor_id` nulled; `inference_log.account_id` nulled and `inference_log.rfp_id` nulled; retained source files destroyed; the email may be reused for a new account); **Stripe is the system of record for payment/billing data (7-year retention); the app's `entitlements` table is operational state that cascades with account deletion**; needs FR-001.

- **FR-028** — Password reset: `POST /forgot-password` with email always returns 200 (prevents account enumeration); if the account exists, a signed single-use token (HMAC-SHA256, ≥32 random bytes, 1-hour TTL) is emailed via SMTP. `POST /reset-password` with token + new password (≥12 chars) updates the hash, revokes all sessions for the account, and marks the token used. **Expired, used, or forged tokens get 410** — a forged token (tampered HMAC signature) returns 410 with no side effect on the account's sessions. Rate-limited to 3 requests per hour per email (FR-030); needs SMTP.

- **FR-029** — CSRF protection: a per-session synchronizer token (stored on the session, rotated on login) is rendered as a hidden field in every state-changing form and sent as the `X-CSRF-Token` header on every htmx PATCH/POST/DELETE. **For JSON API clients (FR-032) that cannot parse HTML hidden fields**, the CSRF token is also returned in the `POST /login` and `POST /signup` JSON response body as `{csrf_token: "..."}`, and a dedicated `GET /api/csrf` endpoint (authenticated, returns `{csrf_token: "..."}`) provides token retrieval for existing sessions. Server-side validation uses constant-time comparison; missing or mismatched tokens return 403. SameSite=Lax is defense-in-depth, not the sole protection; needs FR-001.

- **FR-030** — Rate limiting: uploads 10 per hour per account; `PATCH /api/rows/{id}` 100 per minute per account; checkout 5 per 10 minutes per account; password reset 3 per hour per email; signup 5 per hour per IP (in addition to the FR-002 login throttle). All limits return 429 with a `Retry-After` header; implemented as in-memory token buckets keyed by account/email/IP. **Known limitation:** in-memory limits reset on API process restart and are not shared across API replicas or Uvicorn worker processes. The compose entrypoint pins `uvicorn ... --workers 1` to prevent multi-process bucket multiplication. At beta scale with a single API replica and one Uvicorn worker this is acceptable; if the API is scaled beyond one replica, a durable backend (Redis or Postgres-backed buckets) is required. This is documented as a known limitation, not a silent defect; needs FR-001.

- **FR-031** — Session lifetime: sessions expire after 7 days of inactivity with sliding expiration (each authenticated request refreshes `expires_at`). A session expiring mid-review redirects to `/login` with a return URL; after re-authentication the user lands back on the workbench with all state intact (state is server-side); needs FR-001.

- **FR-032** — Workbench JSON API: `GET /api/matrices/{id}` returns `{id, solicitation_no, state, unlocked, rows: [{id, clause_id, section, page, body, excerpt, response, verified, tags}], filtered_count, audit_drop_count}` for the owner. Without entitlement, `rows` contains exactly the first ten items (or all items if total ≤ 10, same rule as FR-012). 404 for foreign or unknown IDs. `PATCH /api/rows/{id}` (FR-016) is the mutation counterpart. This endpoint lets a future SPA or mobile client consume the same backend without a rewrite. Dedicated acceptance tests (AC-035, AC-036) exercise this endpoint directly, independent of the rendered HTML; needs FR-012.

- **FR-033** — Content-hash re-upload bypass: on intake, if the same account already holds an `rfps` row with the same SHA-256 `content_hash` and `unlocked_at IS NOT NULL`, the new row is created with `unlocked_at` set — entitlement carried over, no payment. **If the prior matrix is in `ready` state**, the existing `requirements`, `filtered_lines`, and `audit_drops` rows are cloned to the new `rfp_id` and no extraction runs — the user sees "This solicitation was previously unlocked — full matrix available" immediately. **If the prior matrix is not `ready`** (failed or still processing), a fresh extraction runs and the UI shows the normal processing state on S5. Applies within the same account only; needs FR-004, FR-015.

- **FR-034** — Noise-filter review panel: S6 displays a collapsible "Review N filtered lines" panel listing each dropped line with page, 80-character preview, **full text (expandable)**, and filter reason. A per-line "Re-include" button (`POST /app/matrices/{id}/filtered/{line_id}/restore`, CSRF-protected) promotes the line into `requirements` at the next `seq` using the stored `full_text`, sets `filtered_lines.re_included=true`, and the re-included line participates in checking and the export lock like any other row. The panel is visible regardless of entitlement; checking and export remain gated; needs FR-009.

- **FR-035** — Continuous quality monitoring: a weekly cron (`QUALITY_CRON`, default Sunday 03:00 UTC) re-runs the FR-025 harness on a rotating 3-RFP subset of the corpus and writes a `quality_runs` row. The harness is also a CI gate on every model/prompt change (and is blocked during FR-043 provider outages). If any metric exceeds the acceptance thresholds, a warning surfaces on S10 `/ops/quality` and in `audit_events`; needs FR-025.

- **FR-036** — Staff bootstrap: `python -m scripts.create_staff --email <email>` creates an `accounts` row with `is_staff=true` and a random 16-character password printed to stdout (changeable via FR-028); idempotent (re-running updates `is_staff` on an existing account); requires `DATABASE_URL` and `APP_SECRET`; run as `docker compose run --rm api python -m scripts.create_staff --email founder@example.com`.

- **FR-037** — Correction logging: every user correction (row un-check, re-include from filtered_lines, re-include from audit_drops, response edit, tag edit) appends to a `corrections` table with `account_id`, `rfp_id`, `requirement_id` (nullable), `correction_type`, `old_value`, `new_value`, `created_at`. This table does not feed back into the model in v1 but provides a replay path for future prompt tuning or fine-tuning; `scripts/export_corrections.py --from <date> --to <date>` exports corrections as JSON. A weekly task (A-018) reviews corrections for patterns that indicate prompt improvements; needs FR-016, FR-034, FR-040.

- **FR-038** — Workbench annotation mode: the founder (staff only) can toggle a "Ground Truth" annotation mode on S6 that exposes per-row buttons: "Correct binding," "Not a requirement," "Add missing requirement." Clicking "Correct binding" on a row writes a `corpus_annotations` entry with the row's `clause_id`, `section`, `page`, `text`, and `is_binding=true`. Clicking "Not a requirement" marks the row with `is_binding=false`. Clicking "Add missing requirement" opens a free-text input for a requirement the model missed; the entry is stored with `is_binding=true` and the founder's text. The resulting entries are exportable in FR-025 ground-truth format via `scripts/export_annotations.py --rfp-id <uuid> --output <path>`, enabling incremental corpus seeding from live extractions without manual 60-hour annotation sessions. The evaluator holdout remains separate (never annotated in the workbench); needs FR-016, FR-036.

- **FR-039** — Per-account inference cap: `MONTHLY_INFERENCE_CAP_USD` (env, default $100) sets a per-account monthly inference spend ceiling. Each model call logs its token counts and estimated cost to `inference_log` with `account_id`, `rfp_id`, `tokens_in`, `tokens_out`, `estimated_cost_usd`, and `created_at`. **Cost estimation formula:** `estimated_cost_usd = (tokens_in / 1,000,000 × MODEL_INPUT_PRICE_PER_M) + (tokens_out / 1,000,000 × MODEL_OUTPUT_PRICE_PER_M)`, where `MODEL_INPUT_PRICE_PER_M` and `MODEL_OUTPUT_PRICE_PER_M` are env vars set to match the configured provider's pricing (e.g., $3/$15 for Claude Sonnet, $2.50/$10 for GPT-4o). When an account's cumulative monthly inference cost exceeds the cap, new uploads for that account are queued at priority 0 (not processed) and the user sees "Your monthly processing limit has been reached — uploads will resume on the 1st" on S3/S4. The cap **resets on the first of each month at 00:00 UTC**. **Sweep-generated jobs (origin='sweep') are exempt from the per-account cap** — see FR-022. This is a hard guard, not an alert; existing entitlements and in-progress jobs are unaffected. The $200/month aggregate infrastructure alert (§8) remains as a system-level guard; **S9 displays a month-to-date inference usage widget** ("This month: $73 of $100 used (estimate)") backed by `inference_log`; needs FR-006, FR-011.

- **FR-040** — Audit-drop review panel: S6 displays a collapsible "Review N audit-dropped lines" panel listing each citation-audit failure with page, excerpt, full text (expandable), and `audit_failure_reason`. A per-line "Re-include" button (`POST /app/matrices/{id}/audit_drops/{line_id}/restore`, CSRF-protected) promotes the line into `requirements` at the next `seq` using the stored `text` — **but first re-runs the FR-008 citation audit on the stored `text` against the cited page**. If the audit passes with a fresh excerpt, the row persists in `requirements` with the verified excerpt. If the audit fails again, the row returns to `audit_drops` with `re_included=true` and an updated `audit_failure_reason`; the user sees "Citation still could not be verified — line returned to audit drops." The re-audit uses the same normalization and fuzzy-match thresholds as FR-008. The panel is visible regardless of entitlement; checking and export remain gated; needs FR-008.

- **FR-041** — ZIP intake and merge: when FR-004 receives a ZIP file, the server extracts its contents, validates that all entries are `.pdf` or `.docx` (any other entry type → 422 "ZIP must contain only PDF or DOCX files"), checks entry count against `MAX_ZIP_ENTRIES` (env, default 500; exceeding → 422 "ZIP contains too many files (max 500)"), and checks for decompression bombs (ratio >100:1 or total extracted size > `MAX_UPLOAD_MB` × 5 → 422). DOCX-to-PDF conversion and PDF merging are performed by the **worker** during FR-005 processing (not during API intake) to avoid cross-container LibreOffice contention — the API stores the raw extracted entries and the worker merges via `pypdf` in filename-sorted order into a single processing PDF. The SHA-256 content hash is computed on the original uploaded ZIP bytes (deterministic; the merged artifact is derived). ZIPs whose uncompressed size exceeds `MAX_UPLOAD_MB`, or that contain encrypted entries, are rejected with 422. **Known limitation:** merge order is filename-sorted, which may not match the logical document order (e.g., `amendment.pdf` may merge before `base_rfp.pdf`). Page numbers in the merged PDF reflect the merge order, not the original document structure. Users who need correct logical ordering should upload the base document separately. This limitation is carried as A-012. Individual attachments within the ZIP are not tracked separately in v1 — the merged stream is processed as one document; the merge is logged in `retention_log`; needs FR-004, FR-005.

- **FR-042** — Amendment linking: the intake form (S4) includes an optional dropdown "Is this an amendment to a previous solicitation?" listing the account's prior matrices by solicitation number and date. Selecting one sets `rfps.amends_rfp_id` to the prior matrix's ID; S3 displays an "Amendment" badge on the linked row. This enables manual linking for future diff views. No automated diffing or delta extraction is provided in v1 — the data model is ready, the feature is not; needs FR-004.

- **FR-043** — Graceful degradation on provider outage: when both model providers (OpenAI and Anthropic) are unreachable (HTTP timeout or 5xx on both), the pipeline enters a **queued-degradation state**: new uploads are accepted and enqueued at priority 10 but not processed; S5 shows "Processing is temporarily paused — your document is queued and will be processed when service resumes"; a banner on S3/S4 explains the degradation; `/healthz` reports `model: "degraded"`; the FR-035 CI harness is blocked (model/prompt changes cannot merge during an outage). A health-probe cron (`PROVIDER_HEALTH_CRON`, default every 30 minutes) pings both providers and auto-resumes processing in priority order when either returns. The state is recorded in `sweep_runs.errors` and surfaced on S10. No data is lost — queued jobs process normally on recovery; needs FR-006, FR-011, FR-024.

- **FR-044** — Daily watchlist digest email: if the nightly sweep (FR-021) produces one or more watchlist matches (FR-022) for the founder's account, a digest email is sent via SMTP to the founder's registered email address. Subject: "[RFP Shred] N new watchlist match(es)". Body: one line per match with solicitation number, title, due date, and a deep link to the sample matrix in S3. If zero matches, no email is sent. The email is sent after all matches for the run are processed (or after `MAX_SWEEP_MATCHES_PER_RUN` is reached). Uses existing SMTP infrastructure (FR-028); needs FR-021, FR-022, SMTP.

- **FR-045** — Source re-trigger: `POST /app/matrices/{id}/reprocess` (CSRF-protected, owner-only) re-triggers extraction from a retained source file within the 24-hour grace period (FR-020). The endpoint is available only when the source file is still present (`stored_path IS NOT NULL`) and the prior run reached `ready` or `failed` state. On success: creates a new `extraction_runs` row (attempt = prior + 1), enqueues a `jobs` row (priority=10), and redirects to S5. If the source file has been destroyed (past 24h grace with `retain_source=false`), returns 410 "Source file no longer available — please re-upload". The S3 library shows a "Re-process" button on rows with `stored_path IS NOT NULL`; S5 shows a "Re-process from source" button when the file is retained. Re-processing does not charge the user; existing entitlements persist; needs FR-020.

## 5. Data, integration, and security contract

ADD VALUE` is irreversible and would break the one-revision rollback promise; CHECK constraints drop and re-add cleanly. All foreign keys specify `ON DELETE` actions explicitly.

**Append-only enforcement.** A PostgreSQL trigger on `audit_events`:
```sql
CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION prevent_audit_modification()
RETURNS trigger AS $$
BEGIN
  RAISE EXCEPTION 'audit_events is append-only';
END;
$$ LANGUAGE plpgsql;

CREATE TRIGGER audit_events_no_update_delete
BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE ON audit_events
FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION prevent_audit_modification();
```

**Index strategy.** `rfps.content_hash` (btree — re-upload bypass lookup). `jobs(status, priority DESC, enqueued_at)` (composite — worker claim query). `requirements(rfp_id, seq)` (composite — ordered retrieval). `sam_notices(naics)` (GIN — watchlist matching). `inference_log(account_id, created_at)` (composite — monthly cap query). `corrections(rfp_id)` (btree — per-matrix review). `corpus_annotations(rfp_id)` (btree — export).

**Durable-state rules.** Accounts, sessions, matrices, requirement rows, filtered lines, audit drops, raw model outputs, corrections, inference logs, corpus annotations, checks, entitlements, sweep history, quality runs, and audit events survive restarts. Uploaded source bytes are ephemeral: 24-hour grace after successful extraction (FR-020), 7-day TTL on abandoned failures, until manual deletion on opt-in. Exports are regenerated per request and never stored. Sweep runs and audit events are append-only. **Stripe is the system of record for payment/billing records (7-year retention).** The app's `entitlements` table is operational state that cascades with account deletion — this is by design, not a contradiction. `rfps.unlocked_at`, once set, never reverts (FR-015).

**Integration boundaries.**
- *SAM.gov opportunities API:* `GET https://api.sam.gov/opportunities/v2/search` with `postedFrom`/`postedTo`, `limit=1000`, `offset`, `api_key`; client-side throttle at `SAM_MAX_RPS`; per-call isolation. **No Entity Management API calls in v1** — vendor outreach is deferred (§1 scope decision). Attachment URLs are extracted from the `resourceLinks` or `responseUrl` field in the response, with fallback to `uiLink` for manual download. The parser handles unrecognized fields gracefully — renamed or unexpected keys are logged as structured warnings and parsing continues. [ASSUMPTION] A-013 covers API terms for commercial use; A-015 covers the daily quota (~10,000 requests/key).
- *Stripe:* Checkout session creation server-side; webhook with signature verification and event-ID idempotency; handled events enumerated in FR-014; both checkout operations wrapped in a single transaction (FR-014); the application never touches raw card data. **Disputes and refunds are not automatically handled in v1** (A-022); the founder monitors via Stripe dashboard.
- *Model provider:* HTTPS JSON behind the FR-011 interface; solicitation text leaves the box only to the configured endpoint; output capped by `MODEL_MAX_TOKENS` per chunk; each call's tokens and estimated cost logged to `inference_log` (FR-039). No automatic failover to secondary adapter (A-023).
- *SMTP:* password reset (FR-028), dunning emails (FR-014), and watchlist digest emails (FR-044). No outreach email in v1.

**Authentication and authorization.** Server-side sessions tracked in the `sessions` table; HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax cookies carry opaque tokens; 7-day sliding lifetime (FR-031). Every object fetch scopes by owner; foreign IDs return 404 — no existence leak, and 404 wording is identical for "foreign" and "nonexistent." PATCH on locked rows returns 404 (FR-016). Authorization check ordering in FR-016: row exists → row belongs to caller → matrix entitled → 404 throughout; only CSRF mismatch returns 403. `/ops/*` requires `is_staff` and 404s otherwise. Reset tokens are HMAC-SHA256 signed (≥32-byte secret), single-scope, and expiring; forged tokens return 410 with no side effect. CSRF synchronizer tokens on all state-changing requests (FR-029); JSON API clients acquire tokens via login response or `GET /api/csrf`.

**Secrets handling.** All secrets (database URL, Stripe keys, API keys, session/HMAC signing secret) injected via environment variables; none committed to version control; `.env.example` lists required names with empty values. Signing secrets are at least 32 bytes. Secrets never logged.

**Validation.** Pydantic schemas at every boundary (forms, webhooks, SAM.gov payloads). Model output must satisfy the FR-007 JSON schema before persistence to `raw_model_outputs`; the transform layer derives `requirements` rows from the stored JSONB, including `clause_id` normalization. Uploads verified by header signature, not extension. Content-Length checked before streaming. Stripe webhook signatures verified with the endpoint secret before processing. CSRF tokens validated on all state-changing requests.

**Privacy.** Solicitation text never enters logs — IDs and content hashes only. No third-party analytics or tracking scripts in the product shell. Minimum PII collected: email address only. Default posture destroys source files after the 24-hour grace (FR-020). No vendor PII is stored in v1.

**Retention.** Matrix rows, filtered lines, audit drops, corrections, and corpus annotations persist with the account. **Stripe retains canonical payment/billing records for 7 years; the app's `entitlements` table is operational and cascades with account deletion.** Audit events 12 months (append-only; `actor_id` nulled on account deletion; trigger prevents UPDATE/DELETE). SAM.gov cached notices refreshed nightly; records older than 30 days purged. Quality runs 12 months. Inference logs 12 months (`account_id` and `rfp_id` nulled on deletion; rows retained for aggregate cost analysis). Raw model outputs 12 months (enables prompt replay). Reset tokens expire after 1 hour. Source files: 24 h after `ready` (default), 7 days after abandonment (failed), until deletion (opt-in).

**Migration.** Alembic manages schema versions; the initial migration creates all tables above including the `prevent_audit_modification` trigger. Forward-only in production with a supported one-revision downgrade (`alembic downgrade -1`) — safe because CHECK constraints and triggers reverse cleanly. No seed data required; the operator account is created via FR-036.

## 8. Deployment and operations contract

**Shape:** one VPS (reference: 4 vCPU / 8 GB, ≈$20/month) running compose services `api` (workers=1, pinned), `worker` (replicas: 1 — enforced in compose to prevent multi-container lock bypass), `sweep`, `postgres`, and `caddy` (automatic TLS). No Redis. Everything else in the budget is model inference and mail.

**Build artifacts:** a single Docker image (~1.5 GB) tagged by commit SHA; the three long-running services (`api`, `worker`, `sweep`) share it with different entrypoint commands (§6).

**Compute resource analysis.** On the reference host at `WORKER_CONCURRENCY=1`: Postgres ~300–500 MB, api ~150–250 MB, worker base ~200 MB, LibreOffice ~500 MB during a (advisory-lock-serialized) conversion, OCRmyPDF/Tesseract up to ~1 GB on large scanned batches. Peak ≈2.5 GB, leaving ~5.5 GB headroom. Raising `WORKER_CONCURRENCY` to 2 is permissible for digital-only workloads; two concurrent LibreOffice/OCR jobs risk CPU starvation and OOM on 4 vCPUs — keep 1 as default. The compose file sets `deploy.replicas: 1` for the worker service; scaling beyond one replica requires a shared lock service (e.g., Redis) for the LibreOffice advisory lock. [ASSUMPTION] If OOM kills appear in container logs, upgrade to 8 vCPU / 16 GB (~$40/month). Observable: OOM events in `docker compose logs`. Validation: founder → monitor during the first two weeks of beta.

**Per-extraction cost model (reconciled token math):** A 200-page digital PDF at ~250 words/page ≈ 50,000 words ≈ 67,000 tokens of text. With ~6,000-char chunks (~1,500 tokens/chunk) and ~500-char overlap, this produces ~45 chunks. System prompt (~300 tokens) plus per-chunk user-prompt overhead (~50–100 tokens) yields ~1,850 input tokens per call → total input ≈ 45 × 1,850 ≈ 83,000 tokens. Output: requirement-bearing chunks (~20–30 of 45) at ~1,000–1,500 output tokens average → ~30,000 output tokens.

| Component | Digital PDF (200pp) | Scanned PDF (200pp, full OCR) |
|---|---|---|
| Input tokens | ~83,000 (45 chunks × ~1,850 incl. prompt overhead) | Same |
| Output tokens | ~30,000 (varies with requirement density) | Same |
| OCR compute | None (local, no model cost) | Local Tesseract — no model cost, only CPU time |
| Claude Sonnet cost (est., $3/M in, $15/M out) | ~$0.70 ($0.25 in + $0.45 out) | Same + local OCR compute |
| GPT-4o cost (est., $2.50/M in, $10/M out) | ~$0.51 ($0.21 in + $0.30 out) | Same + local OCR compute |
| Processing time | ≤5 min | ≤15 min (OCR adds CPU time, not inference cost) |

Scanned RFP inference cost is identical to digital — OCR is local compute (Tesseract/OCRmyPDF), not a model API call. The cost difference between digital and scanned is processing time, not dollars.

**Monthly cost estimate (corrected Stripe fees):** the $149 single-matrix unlock is one transaction per unlock ($149 × 2.9% + $0.30 = $4.62); the $249 monthly plan is one transaction per billing cycle ($249 × 2.9% + $0.30 = $7.52), not per-RFP.

|---|---|---|---|
| VPS | $20 | $20 | $20 |
| Model inference (~$0.70/RFP avg) | $7 | $35 | $70 |
| Stripe fees | $46.20 (10 × $4.62) | $153.64 (30 × $4.62 + 2 × $7.52) | $307.28 (60 × $4.62 + 4 × $7.52) |

Note: Stripe fees are a revenue share, not fixed burn — the moderate column implies ~$5,000 gross revenue against $214 of costs. Pre-revenue burn is the build cost (~$150: VPS 12 weeks $60 + dev inference ~$75 + domain ~$15) plus the first operating month (~$80): **~$230–$400 total before first revenue, far under the $5k walk-away ceiling.** Dev-inference spend during build is bounded at $150 by FR-025 corpus size (10 RFPs) and a build-week iteration cap (≤3 harness runs per RFP) — see A-021. Unlimited-tier break-even: at ~$0.70/RFP inference, a $249/month subscriber would need to process ~355 RFPs/month to consume the subscription in inference; FR-039's per-account cap (`MONTHLY_INFERENCE_CAP_USD`, default $100 ≈ 143 RFPs/month) suspends processing well before that. **S9 shows a month-to-date usage widget** so subscribers can see approaching caps rather than hitting a binary cliff. **Cost ceiling guard:** a $200/month aggregate alert threshold; on breach, reduce sweep frequency and/or switch `MODEL_PROVIDER`/`MODEL_NAME` to a cheaper model (FR-011 config change) and re-run the FR-025 harness to confirm thresholds still pass.

**Backup/restore:** nightly `pg_dump` into `./backups` on the host; restore = `pg_restore` into a fresh volume. Off-site copy via rclone is an [ASSUMPTION]-level default of off (A-006).

**Rollback:** retag the previously deployed image, `up -d`; migrations use VARCHAR + CHECK (not enums), so `alembic downgrade -1` safely accompanies a code rollback when the failing release shipped a migration; the audit trigger is dropped and recreated by the downgrade migration.

**Critical path:** weeks 3–5 (pipeline) is the longest single block; the money phase (6–7) depends on it. The founder alert loop (weeks 8–9) is not on the critical path — if the pipeline slips by a week, the alert loop compresses to one week; if it slips by two, the alert loop is cut and the product ships self-serve only at week 10, with the sweep added post-beta as a config change. Corpus annotation runs in parallel from week 3 onward using the workbench annotation mode (FR-038), not as a blocking pre-build task — but the annotated holdout corpus is a **blocking deliverable for AC-017** at week 12. Two weeks of slack exist across the schedule (10 weeks of build + 2 weeks of hardening/deploy/falsification inside the 12-week window).

Completion is demonstrated by one command sequence: clean clone → `scripts/download_corpus.py` (or `--mock-corpus`) → `docker compose up -d` → `alembic upgrade head` → `scripts.create_staff` → green `pytest -q --cov=app --cov-fail-under=80` → green `pytest tests/api/` → green harness exit on the build-time corpus and the evaluator holdout → green `scripts/smoke.sh` against staging (content-verified). Any seat at the table can run it; the corpus is built in parallel as construction work via FR-038 annotation mode, so nothing waits on anyone's approval — but the evaluator holdout must exist by week 12.
